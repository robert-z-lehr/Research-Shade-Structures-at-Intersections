# [Research: Shade Structures at Intersections](https://robert-z-lehr.github.io/Research-Shade-Structures-at-Intersections/)

- [GitHub Repo](https://github.com/robert-z-lehr/Research-Shade-Structures-at-Intersections/tree/main)

A public reference site and research workspace for evaluating whether shade structures at signalized intersections can be feasible, useful, safe, accessible, and governable as a municipal transportation intervention.

**Status:** preliminary research and reference repository. This repository is not engineering design, construction documentation, legal advice, or a substitute for review by a licensed professional engineer, municipal traffic engineer, ADA/PROWAG reviewer, utility owner, or agency authority having jurisdiction.

## Core Questions

This repository is organized around the practical questions a city, traffic engineering firm, or academic research team is likely to ask before treating shade structures at traffic signals as a viable intervention:

1. Can shade structures realistically attach to traffic signal poles or related intersection infrastructure?
2. What agencies or projects have used related shade, pedestrian-comfort, or right-of-way interventions?
3. What standards, policies, and approval pathways apply?
4. What geometric envelopes are acceptable near sidewalks, curb ramps, crosswalks, signal heads, signs, utilities, and travel lanes?
5. What conflicts must be screened before any pilot is proposed?
6. What evidence would justify a pilot, and what data should be collected before and after implementation?

## Initial Deliverable Goals

A first-stage output is a documented feasibility and screening package, including:

- precedent review,
- standards and constraints matrix,
- site prioritization logic,
- GIS screening layers,
- annotated photos,
- stakeholder interview summaries,
- concept-level geometry assumptions,
- risk and conflict register,
- recommended next-step pilot criteria.

## Example Constraint Categories

Potential constraint categories include:

- MUTCD compliance,
- pedestrian accessibility and PROWAG/ADA considerations,
- utility conflicts,
- signal visibility,
- driver sight distance,
- wind loading,
- maintenance access,
- drainage,
- crashworthiness,
- right-of-way ownership,
- emergency access,
- long-term operations and maintenance.
