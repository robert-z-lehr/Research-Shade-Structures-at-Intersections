# Constraint Matrix

This document tracks major feasibility constraints associated with potential shade structures integrated near signalized intersections and pedestrian infrastructure.

The purpose of this matrix is not to determine final feasibility, but to organize:
- governing constraints,
- responsible stakeholders,
- unknown variables,
- and required future analysis.

---

| Category | Example Question | Governing Source | Risk Level | Unknowns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ADA / PROWAG | Does the structure reduce sidewalk clear width? | PROWAG | High | Geometry |
| Signal Visibility | Could the structure obstruct signal heads? | MUTCD | High | Pole orientation |
| Wind Loading | Does the canopy increase pole moments? | ASCE 7 | High | Material selection |
| Utilities | Are communication or power lines attached? | Utility standards | Medium | Ownership |
| Maintenance Access | Can crews safely access infrastructure? | Municipal operations | Medium | Access envelope |
| Drainage | Could runoff affect pedestrian circulation? | Municipal drainage standards | Medium | Geometry |
| Crashworthiness | Could components become roadside hazards? | FHWA / MASH | High | Structural response |
| Emergency Access | Could the structure interfere with emergency operations? | Municipal review | Medium | Site layout |
| Streetscape Integration | Does the structure conflict with trees or furnishings? | Urban design standards | Medium | Existing conditions |
| Long-Term Maintenance | Who inspects and maintains the structure? | Agency policy | High | Governance structure |

---

# Notes

This matrix is intended to evolve over time.

Some constraints may ultimately prove:
- manageable,
- dominant,
- location-specific,
- or disqualifying.

Additional engineering analysis, agency review, and field validation would be required before treating any candidate concept as deployable infrastructure.
