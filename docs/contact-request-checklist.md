# Contact and Data Request Checklist

## Before contacting anyone
- Verify the person’s current role and preferred contact route.
- Read the most relevant project page, report, or paper.
- State what you understand and mark uncertainty.
- Ask a finite set of answerable questions.
- Explain how the information will be used and cited.
- Avoid sending attachments unless requested; link to a concise public summary.

## ASU SHaDE Lab / Dr. Ariane Middel
- Confirm whether Cool Routes includes signalized-intersection waiting time.
- Ask whether network nodes have dwell costs or only edge-based distance-weighted MRT.
- Ask whether corner-level MRT, signal timing, arrival time, and stationary exposure are planned extensions.
- Request methodological cautions for comparing Phoenix/Tempe with Austin.
- Ask about MaRTy protocols and minimum sampling needed for intersection-scale validation.

## UT Austin / Dr. Dev Niyogi’s team
- Confirm whether CoolPath uses route duration in the thermal objective or only spatial UTCI samples.
- Ask whether signal delay, crossing time, and corner dwell are available in the current architecture.
- Ask about access to Austin UTCI layers, route geometry, model resolution, and update frequency.
- Ask whether an intersection-node module would complement current work.

## Phoenix Sidewalk Shade Project
Request, where available:
1. Exact locations and corner placement.
2. Site-selection criteria.
3. Drawings, dimensions, materials, and foundations.
4. Solar/shadow studies and intended performance hours.
5. Thermal measurements below and beside structures.
6. Pedestrian counts and waiting-location observations.
7. Crossing compliance, conflicts, crashes, or safety review.
8. Accessibility, signal visibility, sight-distance, utility, and drainage review.
9. Capital cost, design cost, maintenance, cleaning, repair, and replacement.
10. Wind/storm performance, vandalism, and complaints.
11. Public-art procurement and artist requirements.
12. Planned evaluation and contacts for unpublished data.

## Austin agencies and partners
- Transportation and Public Works: signals, pedestrian counts, right-of-way, safety, pilot pathway.
- Development Services: temporary structure and permitting pathway.
- CapMetro: high-use stops, rider exposure, shelter standards, and counts.
- UT Austin: campus pedestrian counts, facilities constraints, candidate pilot sites, research permissions.
- Austin Public Health / resilience staff: heat-health framing and vulnerability data.
