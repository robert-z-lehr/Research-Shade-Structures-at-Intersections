# Pedestrian Thermal Exposure at Signalized Intersections

## Purpose
Determine whether modular shade at signalized intersections is a defensible, cost-effective, evidence-based transportation and public-health intervention.

## Core premise
Pedestrians may accumulate substantial radiative heat exposure while stationary at signalized corners. Existing thermal-routing tools appear, from their published descriptions, to emphasize exposure along route segments rather than corner-specific signal waiting. This project tests whether adding time-dependent intersection nodes changes estimated trip exposure, route selection, and intervention priorities.

## Proposed model

`Total exposure = walking exposure + waiting exposure + crossing exposure`

At each intersection, waiting exposure may depend on corner-level MRT or UTCI, arrival time, signal cycle, pedestrian phase, actuation, waiting position, shade geometry, and crowding.

## Research program
1. Literature and precedent synthesis.
2. Standards and feasibility screening.
3. Austin intersection prioritization.
4. Field measurement of thermal conditions, delay, and behavior.
5. Counterfactual shade modeling.
6. Temporary or modular pilot evaluation.
7. Lifecycle cost, maintenance, equity, and decision analysis.

## Evidence labels
- **Verified:** directly supported by a cited source.
- **Inferred:** reasoned from available evidence but not directly demonstrated.
- **Proposed:** a research design, hypothesis, or model extension.
- **Draft:** language or screening assumptions awaiting review.
- **Unknown:** evidence has not yet been located or supplied.

## Candidate Austin screening areas
Initial hypotheses include Guadalupe at 24th, Guadalupe at 21st, Dean Keeton at Speedway, Dean Keeton at Guadalupe, and selected intersections along North Lamar and East Riverside. These are not installation recommendations. Each requires quantitative pedestrian, thermal, signal, safety, accessibility, utility, and right-of-way screening.

## Primary standards families
MUTCD, PROWAG, AASHTO guidance, ASCE/SEI 7, applicable building and temporary-structure codes, and Austin transportation/right-of-way criteria. ASHRAE 55 may inform thermal-comfort concepts but is not the principal outdoor right-of-way compliance standard.

## Pilot outcomes
MRT/UTCI reduction, usable shade duration, person-minutes protected, pedestrian waiting-location choice, route choice, crossing compliance, conflicts, accessibility, cost, installation/removal effort, maintenance, wind/storm performance, and public acceptance.
