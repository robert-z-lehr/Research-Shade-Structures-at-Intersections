# Assessment Framework

## Purpose

This document defines a preliminary screening framework for evaluating whether shade structures near traffic signals and pedestrian crossings may be feasible, useful, and compatible with transportation infrastructure constraints.

The purpose is not to immediately design infrastructure. The purpose is to identify:

1. whether a location is even eligible for consideration,
2. what conflicts may exist,
3. which stakeholders must review the concept,
4. and what additional engineering analysis would be required.

---

# Phase 1 — Context and Need

## Questions

- Is the intersection associated with high pedestrian exposure?
- Are pedestrians exposed to prolonged solar radiation while waiting?
- Is there evidence of heat vulnerability?
- Is the corridor transit-oriented?
- Is the crossing distance long?
- Are there nearby schools, clinics, parks, libraries, transit stops, or senior facilities?

## Example Metrics

- Pedestrian counts
- Delay/wait times
- Shade availability
- Tree canopy coverage
- Land surface temperature
- Air temperature
- Globe temperature
- Wet Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT)
- Mean Radiant Temperature (MRT)
- Heat-related complaint data

---

# Phase 2 — Infrastructure Screening

## Questions

- What pole type exists?
- Who owns the pole?
- What utilities are attached?
- What foundation type exists?
- Is there sufficient clearance?
- Is maintenance access preserved?
- Are sight triangles affected?

## Potential Constraints

- Signal head visibility
- Traffic sign visibility
- Driver sight distance
- Utility attachment restrictions
- Clearance envelopes
- Emergency vehicle conflicts
- Drainage conflicts
- Existing vegetation

---

# Phase 3 — Accessibility and Public Realm

## Questions

- Does the concept preserve ADA/PROWAG accessibility?
- Are sidewalks narrowed below minimum clear widths?
- Are curb ramps obstructed?
- Are detectable warnings affected?
- Does the structure introduce collision hazards?

## Example References

- PROWAG
- ADA Standards for Accessible Design
- Municipal sidewalk standards

---

# Phase 4 — Structural and Environmental Screening

## Questions

- What wind loads may occur?
- Could uplift or vortex shedding become significant?
- Are pole moments increased beyond acceptable levels?
- Is fatigue a concern?
- What happens during severe weather?

## Example References

- ASCE 7
- AASHTO LRFD guidance
- Manufacturer specifications
- Local wind maps

---

# Phase 5 — Safety and Liability

## Questions

- Could the structure become a roadside hazard?
- Does it interfere with maintenance crews?
- Could it fail during extreme weather?
- Does it obstruct cameras or line-of-sight operations?
- Is crashworthiness review required?

## Example References

- FHWA roadside safety guidance
- MASH criteria
- State DOT policies

---

# Phase 6 — Governance and Operations

## Questions

- Which department has authority?
- Who maintains the structure?
- Who pays lifecycle costs?
- What is the inspection schedule?
- Is permitting required?
- Is there an advertising restriction?

## Typical Stakeholders

- City transportation/public works
- Traffic engineering divisions
- Transit agencies
- ADA coordinators
- Utility owners
- Emergency services
- Urban forestry divisions
- Risk management/legal teams

---

# Recommended Initial Outputs

Reasonable early-stage deliverables include:

- GIS screening map,
- constraint matrix,
- precedent review,
- annotated site photographs,
- conceptual geometry studies,
- stakeholder interview summary,
- research bibliography,
- phased pilot framework,
- data collection plan.
